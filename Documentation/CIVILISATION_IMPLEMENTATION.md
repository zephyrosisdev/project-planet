Current milestone implements the foundation and local settlement economy, roads, warehouse buffers, cargo, industrial/oil buildings, crop parcels, housing and population. Later space phases remain required and incomplete. Save APIs cover strategic data only, not complete tactical/GPU saves.

Additional steering: production retains goods locally until collected. Production inputs and food need delivery. Warehouses/markets are placeable. Air cargo bypasses roads. AI uses paid construction, road and vehicle commands. Farming uses batched soil/crop land-use overlays on the sphere. All buildings have food/energy upkeep; each settlement tracks civilian population and housing.

Dormancy supersedes continuously updating all distant economies. Global clock advances, but inactive planets do no farming, economy, transport, population or AI updates. Re-entry applies bounded event batches and paid infrastructure growth. Current scheduler is per planet; same-planet distant settlement filtering is still pending. Catch-up is coarse and event-batched, not tick-for-tick equivalence. Atomic operations can exceed the best-effort time slice. Army state remains GPU-owned and is not replayed on CPU.

Roads: 1x1 catalogue module; spherical cell patches batched without a GameObject per tile. Cardinal masks identify ends, straights, corners, T-junctions and crossings. CPU construction A* lays out paid infrastructure; CPU connected-component labels describe network metadata only. Live cargo routing/motion is performed by SettlementRoad.compute: reverse BFS limited to 128 visited nodes/cart/dispatch, GPU parent chains and GPU pose buffer. Same-owner placed road edges are the only land navigation input. No CPU live route/pose mirror. Ordinary play asynchronously reads 16-byte mission summaries at up to 10 Hz; validation alone can synchronously read those events. Search scratch allocates to current power-of-two road/fleet capacity; configured worst case 32768 nodes x128 slots =64 MiB, excluding small graph/job/pose buffers. No distant cargo dispatch. Offline catch-up uses aggregate mission ETAs instead of live road searches.

Sustained AI supply follow-up: the previous placement/depletion checks missed a long-run economy failure. SettlementDirector now plans at least two material extractors, scales additional extraction when warehouse funds are low and local output buffers are not already backlogged, and expands toward matching deposits with paid outposts. Both strategy planners reserve construction funds; military/research/upgrades wait for an operating HQ-connected bootstrap economy and surplus treasury. Fuel is reserved for cargo, with legacy oil extraction prioritized at low propellant. Power and housing decisions use installed/pending capacity instead of duplicating food-starved buildings. AI road growth roots the primary warehouse and production in the main HQ component; extraction/storage get priority under the existing fair, bounded road budget. Fleet targets include food-consuming outposts/other buildings and can grow up to the configured cargo limit.

Actual cart diagnostics found thousands of construction materials sitting at mines while every cart carried tiny food shipments. Warehouse food collection, construction-income collection and rotating other exports prevent early sources from monopolizing the fleet. Reserved pickup quantities allow multiple construction couriers without promising the same available stock twice; collection still removes only goods actually present. Cargo GPU summaries use individual mission versions instead of discarding a whole readback when any cart changes jobs, and the event polling interval follows tactical simulation ticks. GPU road pathfinding/poses and GPU-authoritative army records remain unchanged. No CPU army mirror or per-road/cart GameObjects were added.

Resource overview and workforce follow-up (17 October 2026): the original RTS toolbar now has RESOURCES, with all 43 resource stocks split into warehouse, local installation and loaded cargo quantities. Building catalogues now specify workers per level. Aggregate population staffs active completed buildings: food/water/farms first, then extraction, then civilian industry, then military/research. Partial staffing scales actual extraction, crop growth, recipes, research, unit queues and cargo manufacturing; no staff stops output. Depleted, paused, unpowered and removed buildings do not retain workers. Houses and automated power do not require workers. Civilian housing is physical capacity, with population migration at one resident per 30 supplied simulation seconds by default; food, housing or electricity shortages stop growth. Resident upkeep scales with population. Workers are bounded building metadata, with no civilian/army GameObject mirror.

The director forecasts jobs including buildings under construction, pays for housing ahead of demand, prioritizes food-chain scaling, and waits for population before optional expansion. The separate military planner also refuses expansion while planned jobs exceed available civilians. Existing paid roads, delivered inputs, cargo fleets and treasury reserves remain in force. Inactive planets still do no local economy/worker updates, and returning catch-up uses the same staffing and growth rules.
