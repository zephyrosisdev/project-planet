# Planet atmospheres and clouds

Planets have independent atmosphere and cloud settings. Generated worlds can be airless, clear, scattered, broken or overcast. Their pressure and breathable-air flags agree with the civilisation environment. Home-planet weather can be edited on its `PlanetDefinition` asset under `weather`: coverage, cloud altitude, thickness, density, wind, haze and colours.

Nearby clouds are actual raymarched three-dimensional density layers: a spacecraft can pass below, through and above them. Volumetric detail fades between 1.5 and 3 atmosphere heights above the surface. More distant planets use the same seeded, wind-driven coverage field on a cheap cloud layer, with progressively filtered noise for small screen sizes. Atmospheric haze and the sunlit planetary limb are rendered separately from cloud coverage, so a clear atmosphere needs no cloud volume.

Weather uses one small 3D noise texture per resident view, shared by all the planets it draws. It follows the existing bounded distant-body visibility list and does not create weather simulation, terrain caches, army buffers or cloud GameObjects for unvisited planets. View render targets are released when a planet becomes inactive.

The renderer integrates atmosphere at six samples and nearby clouds at up to twelve samples per ray, at half resolution. Stable sampling jitter breaks up layer banding, and depth-aware upsampling keeps foreground silhouettes clear. Local scene depth is saved before distant bodies write their compressed visual depths. HDRP can supply this depth as a texture array even outside XR; the weather shader selects the matching texture dimension and decodes depth using the scene projection. Each half-resolution ray stops at the nearest depth in its source footprint, and upsampling never falls back to background clouds over a thin foreground silhouette.

The viewed planet has a 128×64×8 sunlight optical-depth cache. A six-step GPU light march follows the dominant star through the same seeded, wind-driven cloud density, including planet occlusion. Trilinear lookups give cloud interiors attenuated direct light, forward scattering and a broad multiple-scattering approximation. Distant planets use an analytic optical-depth approximation. `lightScattering` controls the blend with the simpler lighting model; this is a bounded approximation, rather than a full multiple-scattering simulation.

`cloudShadowStrength` defaults to 0.18. A ray from each visible opaque surface toward the star finds the cloud layer and samples its filtered optical depth. This applies subtle screen-space attenuation before atmospheric compositing, including on terrain and exterior structures. It follows the dominant star's direction, fades out on the unlit side, and excludes sealed cabin walls. It attenuates the existing opaque colour rather than changing HDRP's individual light contributions; the low strength preserves the scene's ambient appearance. The cache updates with each weather render and allocates no gameplay state or per-unit work.

Cloud rays retain both portions of the spherical layer when they pass below its base and re-enter it. Sampling skips the empty gap while sharing the existing twelve samples across the occupied path. This avoids a circular opacity cutoff at the cloud base without adding raymarch samples or render targets.

At 2560×1440 the weather targets, noise and 256 KiB sunlight cache occupy approximately 21.63 MiB. The million-unit army path, GPU authority, navigation and unit representation are unchanged.

A camera inside a pressurized ship compartment excludes the connected aft and bridge hull volumes from atmospheric and cloud integration. The bounds rotate and rebase with the ship. Opaque cabin walls therefore receive no interior cloud density, while rays through the windshield start rendering weather after leaving the hull. Opening a compartment to vacuum follows the existing hatch/pressure rules. This uses two analytic box intersections only when the camera occupies a pressurized compartment; it creates no additional render targets or weather objects.

## Validation

`-planet-weather-validation` captures weather/control pairs from orbit and inside cloud volumes, plus views below and above scattered clouds, clear atmospheres and an airless world. A foreground opaque mesh below the cloud layer checks that the depth snapshot contains the mesh and that clouds behind it do not alter its interior. It also checks deterministic generation, smooth detail transitions, environment metadata, bounded memory and absence of gameplay terrain allocations for unvisited planets. Paired render requests use an explicit pixel-readback fence; incremental timing includes submission and GPU work, and is not a GPU timestamp. A zero GPU-recorder result is unavailable, not zero cost.

A separate shader fixture renders rays on either side of the cloud-base tangent at 20, 60 and 300 metres above the base. Constant noise isolates the shell geometry. Opacity must agree with an independent 20,000-step density integral and remain continuous across the tangent; the original single-interval renderer fails this check.

The weather fixture also enables the real ship and first-person controllers. It compares weather/control renders against nearby solid-depth samples when facing an actual building, sitting in the pilot seat, facing a cabin wall, and occupying the cabin inside a cloud layer. These solid-surface comparisons disable post-processing to isolate occlusion from bloom. Cloud shadows are disabled for exterior fixtures where shadowing is intentional, but remain enabled inside sealed compartments to verify cabin exclusion. Normal effects are restored for the separate windshield comparison and performance timings. Separate lighting checks compare terrain shadows on/off, verify no shadow on the unlit side, inspect optical-depth variation through the layer, and compare internal scattering on/off. The windshield check verifies that exterior clouds remain visible. When testing in the Editor, verify that scripts have recompiled as well as shaders: a stale renderer assembly can still select the wrong depth-texture variant even when a fresh standalone build passes.

The same standalone AI scenario is measured before and after at 2560×1440 on the RTX 3070 with 4,096 zombies and 4,160 total GPU units. The existing late patrol assertion fails in the baseline; performance comparisons use its completed equivalent measurement phase. No million-unit throughput pass is claimed from this smaller scenario.

The lighting change passes `Weather/LightScatteringVerified/validation.json`, including sealed cabin occlusion with shadows enabled and zero runtime errors. The equivalent weather fixture at 2560×1440 on the RTX 3070 measured incremental fenced weather median/p95 of 0.2862/1.9169 ms before (`Weather/CloudBaseVerified`) and 0.8380/1.8979 ms after. These paired render-request deltas include CPU submission and GPU work, not GPU timestamps. Measured weather texture storage increased from 22,417,993 to 22,680,137 bytes. This cosmetic change does not modify army simulation or claim a new million-unit throughput result.
